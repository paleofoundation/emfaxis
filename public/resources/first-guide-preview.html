The main lesson

Keep the claim attached to the exact exposure, population, outcome, comparison and source limits. If a paper does not answer your question, record the gap.

From chapter one

Before opening a paper, write the decision you are trying to understand in one sentence. “Are EMFs bad?” is too broad to guide this exercise. “Does research on mobile-phone call time answer my question about a wireless device in a bedroom?” gives you something concrete to compare with a study. Add the population and outcome that matter to the question, then leave a separate space for what the paper actually studied. The point is to expose any mismatch before an alarming or reassuring headline becomes your conclusion.
